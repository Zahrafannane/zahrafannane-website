The neuroscience

You can't attract anything from survival mode.

Here's what's actually happening in your brain when you over-function, over-explain, and keep chasing.

A limiting belief isn't just a thought. It's a threat signal. Every time it fires — I'm not enough, I have to earn this, it's not for women like me — your nervous system reads danger and drops into fight or flight. That's the exhaustion. That's the doing-it-all. That's the chase. It isn't ambition; it's a body trying to stay safe.

And a brain in survival can only do one thing: scan for what's familiar. It filters out the opportunity, the person, the money, the ease, because none of it matches what it believes you're allowed. You can want the life as hard as you like. Survival mode won't let it in.

Release the belief and the threat signal stops. The nervous system settles. The brain comes out of fight or flight and into a state that can notice, receive, and act on what it wants — and that is where attracting happens. Not from wanting harder. From no longer being in danger.

The belief is the switch. Flip it, and the whole system follows.
